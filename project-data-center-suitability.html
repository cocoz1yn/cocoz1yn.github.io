Data Center Parcel Suitability Agent: An Explainable Web-Based Screening Tool

This project proposes a web-based AI agent that helps users assess potential data center sites through clear, evidence-based spatial analysis. Using Philadelphia as a pilot area, the prototype will let users select a parcel on a map, choose screening factors, and generate a report explaining the parcel's strengths, concerns, and uncertainties.

In development. The workflow and deliverables below describe the proposed scope.

What the tool will assess

The initial prototype will focus on four factors: flood hazard exposure, distance to power plants, terrain buildability, and community sensitivity through proximity to residential areas and schools.

Flood screening will combine FEMA flood-zone overlap with HAND-based elevation scenarios. Terrain analysis will examine slope and elevation variation, while distance measurements will provide context on nearby power and community features.

An explainable workflow

A Python backend will perform the spatial measurements and calculate factor scores using stated weights. A language model will coordinate restricted geospatial tools and translate their verified outputs into a plain-English report, keeping the underlying evidence visible.

The planned output includes a map, factor-by-factor measurements, an overall screening result, and a downloadable suitability report. Tool results will be checked against manual GIS calculations for a small set of test parcels.

Planned data sources

Philadelphia Department of Records parcel boundaries; FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer; USGS 3D Elevation Program data; EIA-860 power plant locations; and OpenStreetMap residential areas and schools. Dataset sources, dates, quality, and missing information will be recorded.

Scope and limitations

Power plant proximity is a geographic screening measure, not evidence that the grid can supply a data center. The report will flag uncertainty and missing data. Future versions could consider transmission lines, substations, water availability, zoning, land cost, and more detailed flood-risk estimates.
